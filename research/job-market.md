# Quant Developer — Job-Market Research

> A snapshot of the quant-developer hiring landscape to aim the roadmap at
> real demand. **Treat figures as approximate** and re-verify before relying
> on them — compensation and hiring bars move with markets. Revisit quarterly.

## The role vs. adjacent roles

| Role | Primary skill | Typical background |
|------|---------------|--------------------|
| **Quant developer / quant engineer** | Software engineering + quant literacy | CS / engineering, strong coding |
| Quant researcher | Math / statistics / ML | PhD or MS in a quantitative field |
| Quant trader | Markets + fast decision-making | Varied; strong probability/mental math |
| Core/platform engineer | Pure software engineering | CS; may have no finance background |

Quant dev is the most **engineering-accessible** entry point for a strong
programmer — you compete primarily on software skill.

## Where the jobs are

**Buy-side / trading firms**
- Proprietary trading & market makers: Jane Street, Hudson River Trading,
  Citadel Securities, Jump Trading, Optiver, IMC, DRW, Five Rings, SIG.
- Hedge funds: Citadel, Two Sigma, D. E. Shaw, Millennium, Point72, AQR.

**Sell-side / banks**
- Strats and quant-dev desks at large investment banks (markets technology).

**Vendors & infra**
- Trading-tech vendors, exchanges, data providers, and fintechs building
  execution or risk platforms.

Geographic hubs: New York, Chicago, London, Amsterdam, Singapore, Hong Kong,
and growing remote/hybrid options at some firms.

## What they screen for

1. **Coding ability** — fast, correct, clean. Live problem solving in C++
   and/or Python is standard.
2. **Systems knowledge** — concurrency, memory, latency, data structures
   chosen for performance.
3. **Math/probability reasoning** — brainteasers and applied probability.
4. **Domain curiosity** — you don't need to be a trader, but show you
   understand markets and can reason about PnL and risk.
5. **Ownership** — evidence you can build and maintain real systems.

## Compensation (approximate, early-career, top firms)

Compensation at elite prop/HFT firms is high and **bonus-heavy**; banks pay
less but are often more accessible. Ranges vary widely by firm, location, and
level — **verify current numbers** on levels.fyi, Glassdoor, and recent offer
reports before planning around them. The signal that matters: top trading
firms compete aggressively for strong engineers, and the bar is correspondingly
high.

## Common hiring bar / what to prepare

- **LeetCode-style** data-structures/algorithms (medium–hard), timed.
- **Low-latency / systems** design and C++ specifics (RAII, move semantics,
  cache behavior, undefined behavior awareness).
- **Probability** brainteasers: expectation, conditional probability, Markov
  chains, combinatorics, martingales at a basic level.
- **Project deep-dives**: expect to defend design choices in your portfolio.

## How to break in (practical tactics)

- **Build a visible portfolio** (see roadmap Phase 4) — a real backtester and
  a low-latency component carry more weight than coursework.
- **Target internships / new-grad pipelines** if eligible; they're the main
  funnel at top firms.
- **Network**: engineers at these firms, alumni, and quant communities.
  Referrals matter.
- **Open-source / competitions**: contributions and Kaggle-style or trading
  competition results are credible signals.
- **Apply broadly** across the firm types above; the bar and process differ,
  so cast a wide net while tailoring your portfolio story.
